Performance Evidence

Measured facts stay separate from interpretation.

Country / Network Results

For each measurement: location, network/probe where available, timestamp, endpoint, test type, observed latency and relevant timing components.

Sampling rule: averages, ranges and percentile statistics are only reported when the observations justify them. A single observation is explicitly labelled as a point-in-time observation.

Finding Format

Every important finding answers five questions.

What?

What performance condition was observed?

Where?

Which country, network, endpoint or architectural layer is affected?

Why?

What evidence supports the suspected root cause?

What next?

What should engineering change first?

How do we prove it?

What measurement will validate the remediation?

Remediation

Prioritized, not just documented.

Recommendations are ordered using observed impact, confidence in the diagnosis, implementation effort, dependencies and risk. Expected impact is labelled as an estimate unless it has been measured.

Example recommendation

Priority: High

Reduce synchronous dependence on a remote third-party service for a latency-sensitive request path.

Options: caching, asynchronous processing, regional service placement or alternative dependency architecture.

Validation: repeat the same endpoint tests from affected African markets and compare the before/after timing breakdown.

Technical Appendix

Reproducibility matters.

  • Test targets and endpoints
  • Probe locations and networks where available
  • Measurement timestamps
  • HTTP, traceroute and other diagnostic methods used
  • Sampling and aggregation rules
  • Known limitations and unavailable measurements
  • Architecture information supplied by the customer
  • Assumptions and interpretation boundaries
