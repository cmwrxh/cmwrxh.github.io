What This Means

Time-to-first-byte (TTFB) reflects DNS resolution, connection setup (including TLS handshake), and the time your origin server or edge takes to begin responding. A gap of more than roughly 150–200 ms between your fastest and slowest African location usually points to routing distance from origin, missing CDN/edge coverage in that region, or DNS resolving outside the continent rather than application-code issues.

Recommended Next Steps

  • If TTFB is high across all locations: investigate origin server load, backend processing time, and database query latency.
  • If TTFB is high only in specific locations: investigate CDN/edge coverage and DNS geo-steering for that region.
  • If TLS handshake time is high: confirm TLS 1.3 and session resumption are enabled at the edge.
  • Re-run this audit after any change to validate the improvement with the same methodology.
Measurements were taken at a single point in time from public network probes and reflect network and server-response conditions at that moment; results can vary by time of day, ISP, and routing changes. This report presents observed facts (timings) separately from recommendations, which are general guidance rather than a guarantee of a specific outcome.
